Tope Awotona

Founder of Calendly

Why we chose him

Tope Awotona is a Nigerian-born entrepreneur who moved to the United States as a teenager. He studied computer science before switching to management information systems at the University of Georgia. After working in software sales, he founded Calendly in Atlanta in 2013 to make scheduling meetings easier.

Awotona put his personal savings into the business and later received outside investment. He has spoken about investors rejecting his working product while supporting other founders, a difference he attributed to racial bias. In 2021, Calendly announced a $350 million investment round at a valuation exceeding $3 billion.

We chose Awotona because his story challenges narrow expectations about who can lead a technology company and where that company can grow. His career combined an understanding of technology with sales experience and attention to a practical problem.

His connection to equity also deserves scrutiny. Calendly describes employee groups that support Black employees and advocate for accessibility. These are examples of inclusion efforts within the company he founded, although the company’s statements alone do not prove equal outcomes.

For us, his story raises a question about who gets the opportunity to build. Investing personal savings is a risk many talented people cannot afford. Celebrating his achievement should also lead us to question the barriers facing founders who lack that financial cushion.
